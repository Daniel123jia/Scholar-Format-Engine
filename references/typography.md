# Typography rules

## General

- Formatting should be specified by Style Pack, not hard-coded in research skills.
- For bilingual Chinese/English documents, configure both `latin` and `east_asia` font families.
- Use named paragraph styles in DOCX where practical.
- Prefer paragraph-level spacing and indentation rules over manual blank lines.
- Do not use excessive colors; color should communicate hierarchy or semantics.

## Recommended default hierarchy

A neutral research report may use:

- Title: 18–22 pt, bold, centered.
- Heading 1: 15–17 pt, bold.
- Heading 2: 12.5–14 pt, bold.
- Heading 3: 11–12 pt, bold.
- Body: 10.5–11.5 pt.
- Tables/captions: 9–10.5 pt.
- Evidence snippets: slightly smaller than body and visually separated.

These are defaults only. Institutional rules take priority.

## Chinese/English mixing

A common Chinese academic setup is:

- Chinese body: SimSun/宋体.
- Chinese headings: Microsoft YaHei/微软雅黑 or SimHei/黑体.
- English/Latin: Times New Roman.

The actual Style Pack decides the fonts. The renderer writes both East Asian and Latin font mappings so Word can display mixed-script text consistently.

## Paragraphs

Body paragraphs should usually define:

- line spacing;
- first-line indent;
- spacing before/after;
- alignment.

For analysis reports, left alignment is often more readable than forced full justification. For manuscript-like documents, full justification may be selected by the style pack.

## Heading pagination

- Keep headings with the next paragraph when possible.
- Avoid orphaned headings at the bottom of a page.
- Use explicit page breaks only for real structural needs, not as spacing hacks.
